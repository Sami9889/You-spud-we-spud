What is this?

I'm Sami from Hack Club Slack. This is my You Ship, We Ship challenge. Build something small, ship it somewhere public, and if it's good enough I'll send you a parcel from Australia.

Raw potatoes don't survive customs. Stickers, snacks, and a real airmail postcard do. The smaller you build, the better the parcel gets.

You build it, I pack it, Australia Post does the rest.

The rules

Build something small with plain HTML, CSS, and JS. Size matters, but it's not everything - I review every submission myself for quality, originality, and genuine effort.

Fonts and CDN scripts like Google Fonts don't count toward your size limit, but that exemption is just for decoration and styling. Your actual app or game logic has to be your own code, and it counts. That's the size of your real files as they sit on disk, not a gzipped or minified-then-measured number. A blank page, a "hello world" print, or an unmodified starter template doesn't qualify, no matter how small it is.

FAQ

What counts toward the 10KB?

All your files: HTML, CSS, JS, images. External stuff loaded from CDNs like Google Fonts doesn't count, but keep the spirit of it. The point is to show you don't need a million dependencies.

Can I just ship a blank page that says "hello world"?

No. It has to actually do something, even something tiny: a game, a tool, a toy, an experiment. That also rules out unmodified starter templates like the default Vite page or create-react-app splash, and logic loaded from a CDN just to dodge the size limit. Under 10KB is the challenge, not "under 1 line." I check every submission myself.

Can I use AI tools like Copilot or ChatGPT?

For debugging and fixing a specific issue, sure. For generating your actual app or game logic all at once, no. The core idea and code need to be yours - that's the spirit of the challenge.

Does it have to be a website?

It needs to run in a browser: a site, game, tool, interactive thing. No native apps. Beyond that, build whatever you want.

What counts as a real project?

A 1KB page built in 2 hours from a tutorial won't pass review. It needs to be something you actually designed and built: a game with original mechanics, a tool that solves a real problem, an experiment with your own ideas. Copy-paste tutorials, unmodified starter templates, and trivially similar clones of existing projects don't qualify.

How are submissions reviewed?

By me, not just by size. Every submission is checked for completeness, originality, and genuine effort. Size is the starting line, not the finish line. If it looks like a tutorial output, a stub, or a template, it won't pass.

Does it have to be potato themed?

No, but it earns bonus points.

Is the parcel really more than potatoes?

Yes. Potatoes only show up in Ultra Hard Mode. Everyone else gets stickers, a postcard, snacks, and whatever else fits in the envelope that week. On top of that, only the top-ranked submissions per tier receive the full physical parcel - the rest get a public shoutout and recognition on the site.

How long does shipping take?

It's coming from Australia so a few weeks depending on your country. It will arrive.
